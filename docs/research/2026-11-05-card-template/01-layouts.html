Postcard template

One 2:3 card at every size. A black border the engine draws in the card's color, badges that sit on its inner edge, and an inner canvas the art owns. Default art keeps to the focus zone and may glow past it. A full-art variant fills the whole canvas.

Anatomy

The guides show what an artist works to. The engine clips at the border and nowhere else.

Card600 × 900The template's units. 2:3 at every size: slot 86, phone 104, fan 150 and sheet 276 wide.
Border30 · r 30Black, ringed and glowing in the card's color. The engine draws it, so no art file contains it.
Canvas540 × 840 @ 30,30 · r 15The inner area, and the art file's whole canvas. The only clip.
Focus zone460 × 400 @ 70,100Where a default art's subject sits, on a transparent ground. Glow, rings and splashes may run past it. A guide, never a mask.
Plate zonelower thirdAlways covered by the name plate and the text plate, which are translucent glass. Art there reads as texture behind words.
Border pieceskeep clear 12Alignment and play (parallelograms), rarity (a gem on its point), price, and the foot line with the artist's credit, all from the card's data. They may cover the border's inner part, never its outer edge: every piece stays 12 units inside the silhouette (the dotted line).
Mark96 × 96 · live 72A separate square silhouette, drawn to read at 18px inside the post's mark tile.

The art set for a new card: color and ink, default art (transparent, focus zone), and a mark. A full-art variant is another canvas of the same size with no restraint.

Three layouts

Each one borrows a convention a TCG player already knows, set in the glass's shapes. Shown at the fan's size, default art and full art.

Every size

The same card, scaled. A smaller size drops pieces. It never reshapes the art.

The mark

On the board, a played card is its mark on the post's date row. The mark is its own drawing, a silhouette that holds up at 18px.
