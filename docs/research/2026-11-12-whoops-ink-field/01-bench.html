What to look for

  • Where a pen line leaves the splat, the field version swells into it like one of the splat's own arms. Today's line stops flat at the edge.
  • A pen line written inside a washed hole picks up the same bright rim and darker far side as the splat's edge.
  • Over ink of its own color, a pen line either stands as a raised ridge that catches the light ("Raised ridge") or vanishes into the ink, as the same ink would ("Melts in"). Today it shows as a flat line.
  • Two pen lines that cross pool a little at the crossing. "Joins" sets how far that pooling reaches.
  • "Wash takes": with "All of it in one pass" a wash clears everything under the brush, as today. With "Some of it each pass" each pass takes a fixed amount of ink, so the splat's thin arms clear at once and its thick middle needs scrubbing back and forth; thinned ink turns see-through as it goes.
  • "Wet rim" gives a wash's edge the meniscus too. "Dry cut" keeps today's flat cut for washes and shades only the pen.
  • "Bulb" ends every pen line in a drop. "Flicked drops" throws one to three droplets past the end when the last stretch of the line was fast. Both are worked out from the saved points alone, so every reader sees the same ones.
  • Pale inks (gold, white) get the darker rim the splat already uses, so pen ink stays visible on the white page.
