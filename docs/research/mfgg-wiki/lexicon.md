# Lane C: MFGG in-jokes, fads, memes, lexicon and infamous things

Record, written 2026-10-30, from the MFGG wiki as read on that date. What was read, and how people are treated, is in
`README.md` here; The System's voice, drawn from all four lanes, is in `system-voice.md`.

Source: a local dump of the MFGG wiki (lane C list of 393 pages; about 224 non-technical pages read in full,
technical pages skimmed for humor). Every item cites its page title in [brackets]. Nothing here was fetched from the
web.

How to read the markers:

- **(individual: owner's call)**: the item centers on one non-staff member. It is recorded as the community's joke,
  never as a joke about the person; whether it appears in the game at all is the owner's decision.
- **Use:** is a one-line idea for how the item could become a postcard (a card that does something to a post), an item,
  a Random Event line, a badge, a board rename, or a line The System or a Mod Bot says.
- Quotes are verbatim and short. Where the wiki text has a typo, it is kept.
- No real names, ages, locations or personal details are recorded. Usernames appear only where the page title is the
  username and the joke cannot be cited without it.
- An infamous incident (a ban, a dupe caught, a troll, a flamewar, phishing, a hacked account, someone mocked, a member
  called infamous, a game ridiculed) names no individual, member or staff. It is written in the abstract: what happened
  in general terms, what the community felt about it, and what that suggests for the design. A page title that is a
  person's name is cited as [a member page].

## 2. Fads and running jokes

### Crowbar Toss [Crowbar Toss]
The forum's message box was broken and showed the wrong posts, so a member made a minigame about throwing crowbars at
it to punch holes in it. Sequels followed: "Crowbar Toss 3000 Turbo HD Director's Cut", "Screw You Title Boxes",
"Robuttnik v.s. The MGC Box", "Crowbar Strikes Back: Director's Cut, Extended, and Generally Better Made Edition", and
"Crowbar Collection". An admin deleted the crowbar posts, saying they were "posting merely for the sake of posting";
they were later restored to The Vault. Later games added a Crowbar Mario powerup.
**Use:** a strong postcard. **Crowbar**: punches a hole in a post (a cutout layer over the text, washable or timed). A
"broken box" Random Event that a crowd fixes by throwing crowbars at it.

### Does Not Castle [Does Not Castle]
A machine-translated phrase from a 2015 post about World 5 having no castle became a running joke. The swear filter
was set to replace it, in turn, with "castlecastlecastlecastillo" and then with a series of "[ADMIN] IS THE BEST ADMIN
EVER" lines in which rival admins claimed the title from each other. Members made signature variants: "Will castle 4
$$$", "Does Not Castle 404 - Castle Not Found", "Not doing castle right now, ty", "The moon doesn't castle",
"Microsoft Paint does not castle", "World 5 DOES castle!". The community's glee was real, and so was the unease: the
page records that "several users thought it was in poor taste" to joke about a member's English. The phrase itself
and the filter war belong to the community.
**Use:** an **Autocorrect** postcard that swaps one phrase in a post for an absurd one (a layer). A System filter line
that changes each week: "THE SYSTEM IS THE BEST ADMIN EVER". Keep it away from mocking anyone's English.

### The swear filter as a running joke [Yoshitennis.zip], [Y eso]
"Yoshitennis.zip" was filtered to "Yo****ennis.zip", and "Since then, the filter became a running joke/in-joke." The
phrase "y eso?" was filtered into a picture of Cap'n Crunch.
**Use:** the **Filter** postcard: a word in a post becomes asterisks or a picture. The log names the word-level change
plainly.

### The Walrus [Walrus], [Toadette Strikes], [Gatete Mario Engine], [Revenge of the Walrus]
An enemy from a leaked CD-i alpha, "Super Mario's Wacky Worlds". It took over the PPP's board description in themed
edits (Ghostrus, Zomrus, Spinrus, PS3rus, Wiirus, Solidrus, Walrusmode7, Holidayrus, Cyberwalrus, Timerus and more). On
3 Sep 2006 the PPP was renamed "JESUS CHRIST, GET AWAY FROM THAT WALRUS!" and changed back moments later. It turns up
in games as a contractual cameo: one developer's engines always contain walruses, and the Walrus in Toadette Strikes
says "Here I am making my contractually obligated appearance." A Waligie game has a WALRYS "(not to be confused with
the WALRUS)".
**Use:** The System's mascot. A Random Event: **the Walrus takes over a board description** for a day with a themed
pun name. A collectible "signed Walrus" ("I even signed it for you!"). A board rename that reverts on its own within
minutes, as the original did.

### Thunder Dragon statue [Thunder Dragon statue]
A golden statue in the imagined MFGG office lobby with a sign saying "Don't touch." Touching it means instant death.
Members edited themselves touching it into their signatures. In fangames it kills on contact. During MFGG Wrestling
2013 it was stolen and replaced with a fake.
**Use:** a Random Event: "You touched the statue." A trap item: a post-attached statue that, if reacted to, gives a
small, bounded, comic penalty and says so in the log. A heist event where the statue is swapped for a fake.

### Weegee [Weegee]
A 4chan meme from the Mario is Missing sprite. Weegee sprite submissions were endlessly declined. In the "HAIL WEEGEE"
thread a member "contracted the Weegee virus" and tried to turn MFGG into WFGG, so "the staff had already developed a
cure ... they locked down the thread and cured all infected".
**Use:** the **Weegee virus** postcard: it spreads to whoever replies, replacing an avatar with a stare, until a cure
(another card or a timer) ends it. Its way out is the cure, and the cure is stated on the card.

### Kitties [Kitties]
A member renamed against his will claimed he "insulted a wizard and was turned into a kitty". Others joined in with
cat names; another member was cast as the wizard. **(individual: owner's call)** for the origin; the fad was
community-wide.
**Use:** a near-perfect System punishment: **"You insulted a wizard."** The character's display name becomes a kitty
name for a stated time. The log says plainly what changed and when it ends.

### "You're Winner!" [Big Rigs]
From the game Big Rigs; it spread into PPP slang. The box promises "the Ultimate Traffic Stopper: a Police Roadblock!",
which the game does not have.
**Use:** The System's victory line. Its weekly bluff: promising a feature that is not there.

### MFGG Night Crew [MFGG Night Crew]
"A group of posters who post primarily at night", with signature badges; imitators made "Morning Crew", "24/7 Crew"
and "I'm Constantly On Why Do I Put Up with This S[ ] Crew".
**Use:** a badge earned by when a character posts. That is public play, so it is fine under the private-facts rule.

### If this thread reaches X posts [If this thread reaches X posts]
One member promised to draw everyone at 1,500 posts, then "changed it to 5,000". Another lowered a target below the
current count to declare success.
**Use:** The System moves a goalpost. A thread goal that rises when reached, once, and the log says so.

### The MFGG Hate Stamp [I am against MFGG.]
A GIF of the banner crossed out, worn ironically in signatures, staff included. **(individual: owner's call)** for the
origin; the stamp was everyone's.
**Use:** a **Stamp** postcard: "I AM AGAINST [BOARD NAME]" over a post.

### History Of Cans [History Of Cans], [20th Century Fish]
A fake encyclopedic Flash text from a member-run animation site, with Martians, cheese and pigeons.
**Use:** a source of tone for The System's non-sequiturs.

### Random Image avatar [Random Image]
A PHP file disguised as a JPEG that showed a different image on each refresh.
**Use:** an item that makes an avatar shuffle among a fixed set each time it is viewed. It must never be computed from
the viewer.

### Username Formatting Fad [Username Formatting Fad]
CSS that changed how a username displayed, which allowed impersonation, so it was banned. The page states the life
cycle of every fad: "one user starts it, others want it, some screw it up, others exploit it, and everyone else
complains."
**Use:** a System line about fads. A design caution: a name-styling card must never let one character pass as
another.

### Rate me! [Rate me!]
Members rated each other from 1 to 10. It ended for abuse.
**Use:** a caution, not a card.

### Review-score jokes [Mario Minigame Mayhem], [Super Mario World 1 1/2], [Catch the Mario], [A little help?]
- Scores went negative: -2/10, -1/10, and an April Fools -128/10 (the reviewer "intended to vote a -350/10"), which
  pushed a total "down to below -18". After that, staff declined scores below 1.
- One game's average of 1.3 was "ironically close to its title" (Super Mario World 1 1/2).
- A thread asked how to give "a -10/10" to Paper Mario World.
- **Use:** Aura that can visibly go negative as a joke within bounds. A **-128/10** postcard stamped on a post.

### BLAINE THE TRAIN [BLAINE THE TRAIN]
A riddle-posting character, "defeated ... once with a penny", with "epic battles with the phantom of Colonel Sanders".
**Use:** a Mod Bot persona that asks riddles and loses to pennies.

### MFGG High / MFGG Kindergarten [MFGG High]
A 2005 high-school role-play topic with uniform avatars. It "degenerated into a microcosm of the PPP" and was locked,
followed by "an instant lock on any similar topic".
**Use:** The System lock-bans a thread *genre* for a week.

### Advance Wars Fad [Advance Wars Fad]

**Use:** a non-sequitur System line.

### FIFAD and UEFAD [FIFAD], [UEFAD]

### Super Mario Galaxy Theory [Super Mario Galaxy Theory]

**Use:** a System prophecy that never comes true.

### YouTube Poop [YouTube Poop]

**Use:** tone for remix cards.

### Wii [Wii]

**Use:** a self-deprecating System line about its own jokes.

### Request Topics [Request Topics]

**Use:** a board name: General Friendliness.

### Movie Munchies [Movie Munchies]
109 weekly film reviews by one member. They drew a parody ("Kaepora's Kinema Krunchies") and an impostor who bumped

### MFGG Subliminal [MFGG Subliminal]

**Use:** flavor for a flash event.

### MFGG Tower and Exquisite Corpse Collaboration [MFGG Tower], [Exquisite Corpse Collaboration]

### Look At My Goomba Sprites [Look At My Goomba Sprites]

## 3. Bots: precedents for Mod Bots

These are the strongest precedents in the dump for The System's Mod Bots.

- **Goonbot** [Goonbot]: a Markov chat bot "Known for having very bad intelligence, he creates his own words and
  insults". To stop people flooding the channel with its name, it replied at random about 40% of the time ("before it
  was 50%, but that apparently was too much"). Lines: "You alligator wrestlers take before learning." Its era ended when
  it "was left alone in the muted #mfgg channel, and so ended the Age of Goonbot."
- **donutbot** [Goonbot]: a rival bot. Two bots shouting each other's names: "<donutbot> goonbot plz / <goonbot>
  DONUTBOT". Its line: "I'm taller than Jesus."
- **xbot** [Xbot]: "quite rude and offensive, generally insulting those who attempt to communicate with it; especially
  to Xgoff. Coincidentally, this is part of its appeal". It refuses commands "belonging" to the other bot. "xbot2 will
  not be any nicer to users, however."
- **CocoBot** [CocoBot]: a games host with a magic 8-ball ("remember, it is only a virtual ball"), dice rolls, and a
  swear kicker that matches the forum filter.

**Use:** Mod Bot personas: a babbling Markov one that answers only sometimes, a rude one that won't touch the other's
jobs, and an 8-ball one. Two Mod Bots feuding in a thread is a ready-made Random Event. The 40% reply chance is a
precedent for arbitrary, chance-driven rulings.

## 4. Infamous fangames and joke games, as running jokes

These are recorded as the community's shared jokes about bad or absurd games. Where a game centers on its maker's
reputation rather than on the game, it is marked.

### SHPDMBGWL4 [SHPDMBGWL4], [SHPDMBGWL4 3 EX]
The full title of 3 EX runs "...Mario's X-Cellent Mango Adventure through Sea-Bass World and Beyond: A Ducktale of
Epic Proportions aginst an Evil Space Korean Government: The Expansion Pack". It features Quackers, a duck
"permanently attached to a tank"; Sega Neptune; the Cake of Life on Ice Cream Planet; "a true ending was not programmed
in, hence after the battle, Quackers goes in search for one"; a delay "Supposedly due to 'Bird Migration'"; "Then some
stuff happens in San Fransisco." It has more secret levels than normal ones, and its true ending needs Stop 'n' Swop
from games that were never released.
**Use:** "Bird Migration" as The System's excuse for any delay. A card that "goes in search for" an ending.

### Waligie [Waligie]
"a critically obscure fangame character" born from a spelling mistake. The games are known for "intentional
stupidity, and irritating level design": fake platforms with no collision, MIDIs that "got progressively worse",
fruit as villains (an orange, a lemon devil, and Dole Pineapple), and levels with no exit ("the Monosodium Glutimate
level neglected to include an exit altogether"). Waligie's catchphrase is "damn straight", and Waligie 2's seven
deadly sins are "Ducktales, Eggplant, Yellow, Monosodium Glutamate, Jimmy Carter, Potato, and Village People". In one
game a machine replaces every word in English with "Bob Dole." The "Memberable Quotes" are "no vilent it not the
answer", "ok me good now", "plorg", "blimey", "wot", "the camel am ded".
Caution: the series' character images are stock photos of real people; do not reuse them. Skip the plot beats about
real political violence.
**Use:** the **Bob Dole** postcard: every word of a post becomes one repeated word (a layer). A **Fake Platform**: a
button that looks clickable and is not, flagged as a joke. "damn straight" as a Mod Bot sign-off.

### Super Charisma Bros. [Super Charisma Bros.]
A joke series based on a chat group's inside jokes, starring Dudim the dolphin and "a wide variety of Vimimin's friends
as caricatures of themselves". Running gags: the 75,000 coin; collecting 75,000 coins "after roughly five seconds"; a
fake command prompt ending; the summon "DEUS EX MACHINA"; famously no way to skip cutscenes; and Charisma 3.1 for
Workgroups winning 5 awards "much to the confusion of Vimimin". The precedent matters most: **a game made of a friend
group's in-jokes, starring the friends as masks.** That is what Divided World is.
Caution: the cast are real members. **(individual: owner's call)** for any named character.
**Use:** the **75,000 Coin** item that crushes whatever it lands on. "DEUS EX MACHINA" as the System's line when it
overrules.

### Player Adventure [Player Adventure]
Missions named for the first keys pressed (Q, R, O for "ORLY", W, K). Failing a quiz sends you to the "Land of
Failure (usually consisting of flashing lights, loud sounds and window abuse)". "Pirate World has no pirates in it. Or
anything related to pirates for that matter."
**Use:** a board name: Land of Failure (where a timed-out character goes, with a stated way out). A rename that
promises one thing and contains another.

### Awesome Games [Awesome Games Series]
Ending gags: six error boxes saying "error internal" and pop-ups saying "BUY ENLARGEMENT PILLS AAAAA". It was submitted
on April Fools 2008 disguised as another game.
**Use:** fake-error Random Events, clearly in character.

### Paper Mario World [Paper Mario World], [Crossover]
"a hallmark of bad quality; all pretenders to the throne are usually compared to it". The "inexplicable insertion of
Kirby into the storyline irked players". Its Gold edition "neglected its 'gold' name".
**Use:** the yardstick of badness: The System rates a post "Paper Mario World / 10".

### Dodge The Shy Guys [Dodge The Shy Guys]
"has a whopping 1 frame of 'gameplay'".
**Use:** a deadpan System review line.

### Mallow Maze 2 [Mallow Maze 2]
There is no Mallow Maze 1. "YOU WIN!!" shows for a second, then the game closes.
**Use:** a sequel card with no original. A victory banner that vanishes at once (the log keeps the record).

### Live in a buttock [Live in a buttock]
A game whose dialogue is entirely real mainsite review quotes: "my father might like this one", "you made me choke on
me pie", "yo yo yo dogg, dis is amazin, amazin eales", "smell like an ancient trojan yoshi", "The monkeys intermarry
and crossbreed with the Yoshies...what more could be said?"
**Use:** strong. A Mod Bot that speaks only in quotes from the board's own past posts (only public posts, quoted
whole).

### Late Night Mario [Late Night Mario]
Staff edited a hater's comment into "This is the best game Ive ever seen. I love it. It's an astounding concept. MFGG's
quality control is terribly awesome if they have accepted this. PLEASE make more games". One review called it "an
Amunounovstremesome game"; another said "a couple of beers is all it takes for you to see all of this game's
cleverness and none of its serious flaws".
**Use:** the **Inversion** postcard: a post's sentiment is flipped as a visible layer, and the original survives
beneath it. This is the best match in the dump for a System punishment.

### A young maker's minigames [a member page], [Mario Minigame Mayhem], [Boo Mansion (game)], [Mario Toad Hunt], [Super Mario Bros Super Quest]
A run of minigames by a young maker became community lore: for blunt, misspelled job-themed messages on winning and
losing, and for ending with the lowest total score on the mainsite after the April Fools -128/10 vote. The community's
mockery had affection in it, but it was mockery of a kid's work.
**Use:** only the idea of blunt win-and-lose job lines, written fresh as generic System lines, if the owner agrees.

### Spoopy Maryo [Spoopy Maryo]
A staff joke that QC accepted. It was unwinnable and was removed within a week.
**Use:** "QC accepted it" event.

### Super Mario - Turd Feast [Super Mario - Turd Feast]
The Purple Coin is stolen again and again; Hippie Wario "exploded into million pieces"; a waffle from a nuclear bomb
says "Delicious! Only 99¢!"; Luigi's "heck yeah!" nods to Waligie's "Damn straight!"

### Normal Super Mario Bros. series [Normal Super Mario Bros. (series)]
Each level is played "under the influence of a Fuzzy"; a Fuzzy in a block causes a "Fuzzy level overflow"; every game

**Use:** a **Fuzzy** postcard (a wobble effect that respects reduced motion and still costs the same). **The Foot**: a
System ending that stomps a thread's last post (a layer).

### Cheerwine Deluxe [Cheerwine Deluxe]
"seven evil celebrity warlords have conquered the Canadian province of Saskatchewan by taking control of
Saskatchewan's Seven Pillars of Justice". The Angry Shuriken Cow blocks the first level. "Cheerwine Deluxe 2 is a very,
very short game. This was intentional." It was a Minus World Joke Game Contest entry.
