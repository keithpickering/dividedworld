# Lane D: member and game pages (MFGG wiki dump)

Record, written 2026-10-30, from the MFGG wiki as read on that date. What was read, and how people are treated, is in
`README.md` here; The System's voice, drawn from all four lanes, is in `system-voice.md`.

Source: the 1,030 member and game pages in a local dump of the MFGG wiki. Every item names the page title(s) it comes
from. Quotes are verbatim and kept under about 40 words. Each item ends with **Use:**, a one-line idea for turning it
into a card, item, Random Event line, badge, board name or line The System says. Items that center on one non-staff
member are marked *(individual: owner's call)*. Pages owned by other lanes (Walrus, Fake Promotion, Greek Member, April
Fools, Soveryhappy, the 2002 flamewar, Paper Mario World and others) are mentioned only where they come up on a member
or game page.

An infamous incident (a ban, a dupe caught, a troll, a flamewar, phishing, a hacked account, someone mocked, a member
called infamous, a game ridiculed) names no individual, member or staff, and quotes nothing that would identify one.
It is written in the abstract: what happened in general terms, what the community felt about it, and what that
suggests for the design. Where the source page's title is a person's name, it is cited as (a member page). Event
accounts are described by what they did, not by their names.

Privacy: this file holds lore and events, not biographies. No real names, ages, places, schools, jobs, relationships,
health details or life outside the forum appear here. The "Privacy exclusions" section at the end lists what was left
out and why, without repeating any of it.

## 1. Admin voice and punishments as events

### The Big Red Sig
An admin replaced a member's oversized signature image with red capitals written in that member's own voice: a
confession of the offense, and a warning of the long ban that would follow a second time. The same admin was
remembered for long bans for minor infractions, each sealed with a red signature of this kind. Members then copied the
look until it became a fad. The community's feeling was mixed: the sentences felt harsh, but the voice was funny
enough that wearing it voluntarily became a joke and then a badge. (member pages)
**Use:** a punishment card, "Big Red Sig", that lays a confession in red capitals over a character's signature, written
by The System in the character's own voice. It is a layer, so the original signature survives. The fad that followed
suggests a second effect: other characters may copy the red sig voluntarily, as a badge of honor.

### The imposed-name policy
In 2007 an admin answered every name-change request by giving the member a name he picked himself. At least one member
kept the name he was given. (member pages)
**Use:** a card, "Petition for a New Name". The System grants the rename, but chooses the name. A later page has the
straight version of this: a member who disliked his new name asked an admin to change it back, and he did (a member
page). That gives the card a way out, so nobody is stuck with the name.

### Sentences with units misread
An admin handed out 15 to 20 day bans for posting after him in one topic. When a member asked for a 3-hour ban, the
admin replied "3 months", and the ban was 3 months. The wiki closes the matter with: "This is all that shall be said of
the particular event." The community remembered it as a joke with an edge of injustice: a request granted to the
letter, and against the one who asked. (member pages)
**Use:** an Appeal outcome, "Unit Error". The System grants what was asked for, in the wrong unit (hours become
months). It needs a short cap and a stated end, because no player is ever stuck. A good Random Event line: "This is all
that shall be said of the particular event."

### The nonsense warn
A staff member gave silly warns with absurd reasons, for example one for investigating a case without letting a famous
fictional defense attorney help. The warned were more amused than wronged, because the reason was plainly a joke.
(a member page)
**Use:** a table of absurd warn reasons for The System to draw from. The in-character reason can be nonsense, but the
player's log must still name the real Act and its effect plainly.

### The warn meter and "technical difficulties"
During a quoting bandwagon, one member was given a small warn for quoting a reaction GIF. That took him to 100% and a
year's ban. Staff blamed "technical difficulties" for not lifting the ban, and he was unbanned in 2010 after a review.
What the community took from it was a sense of injustice: a tiny last step, an outsized sentence, and an excuse that
sounded like a shrug. (member pages)
**Use:** a warn meter shown in percent, where a small last increment tips a character over the line. "Technical
difficulties" works as an excuse The System gives in character. The log must still say what actually happened.

### Uneven sentences for one fad
The page-flip HTML fad ended with three people banned for 2 days each, and their sentences were not applied evenly,
which the members who joined the fad remembered as arbitrary. (a member page)
**Use:** a Random Event, "Crackdown". The System picks some of the characters who joined a fad, not all of them, and
punishes those it picked.

### Self-requested permabans
Several members asked to be permabanned. In one case the request was said to have been coerced, so the ban was moved
onto the one accused of coercing it, and then lifted for lack of evidence. The suspicion outlasted the ban. (member
pages)
**Use:** a card, "Request a Permaban". The System may grant it, move it onto another character, or refuse. Whatever it
does, the ban needs a stated way out.

### Klobber's Hell
Klobber turned the Pointless Post Palace into Hell, "complete with flaming Satanism symbols and Parakarry with a
shotgun". (Klobber)
**Use:** a Hell skin for a board during the weekly turnover.

### The stock verdict
A moderator locked a sprite-theft thread with a curt "Yep, busted" and one line saying the sprites were plainly edits
of the originals. The bluntness was the satisfying part: a catch announced in two words. (member pages)
**Use:** "Yep, busted" makes a good stock line for when The System rules against a character.

### Black Squirrel's graphs and decrees
Black Squirrel took office as admin with "a very long acceptance speech with all sorts of pointless graphs" and posted
monthly news with charts he made himself. He pushed "for a more autocratic and less democratic community", and he
renamed the CCC "Casual Time Battle 3", with a Walrus in place of Mario in the board's description.
> "MFGG's forums are like a roller coaster. There's ups and downs... and lefts and rights... and a corkscrew, and a
> sweaty, underpaid worker running the thing..." (Black Squirrel; Mario's Time Battle 3)

**Use:** this is the template for The System's weekly turnover announcement: a decree with a pointless chart, and a
board renamed with a small swap in its description. The roller-coaster quote suits The System talking about itself.

### Retriever II: the Green Button, the 20-page tax and the missing Retriever I
Retriever II ran the server and was jokingly blamed whenever the boards broke. He coded "The Green Button" so users
could fix problems themselves. One April Fools he got back at ShadowMan by breaking the board so that ShadowMan
thought he had done it. He also warned that every topic fad would cost the PPP 20 pages: "If they ran out of pages,
they would apparently lose." Rob still mocks fads by posting "20 pages". As for the first Retriever: "Was he killed by
Retriever II? Was he lost at sea? The world may never know." (Retriever II; Rob)
**Use:** three ideas.
- "The Green Button" as an item. It does something, but the item never says what.
- A "20 pages" fad tax as a Random Event. Each fad thread costs its board pages, and a board that runs out "loses".
  Losing must have a way back, such as the next turnover.
- Retriever I as standing lore for The System to hint at and never explain.

### ShadowMan's events and the fake release
ShadowMan made "over-the-top April Fool's Pranks every April 1st" and ran events with names like ITF we are muscular
trapeze artists, Stupidity Discharge, A Day That Will Live in Infamy and Zombie Week. He posted the History Of Cans.
On April 1, 2008 he faked a Super Mario Theta release that sent a black hole across the screen. He told one new
moderator "he'll regret being a moderator and hate himself after four days." He nicknamed a game delayed again and
again "Half-Life Epic 2". (ShadowMan; Super Mario Theta; Joey; Super Mario Epic 2 Dream Machine)
**Use:** those event names make a ready pool of names for The System's Random Events. The fake release with a black
hole suits an April 1 event. The "four days" line suits The System welcoming a character who gains Mod Bot favor.

### Petitions and pressure
A strict moderator became the target of protests, including a whole subforum on an outside site, and ended up as the
final boss of a member's fan story. A former staff member refused to come back until the moderator was removed, and is
said to have started a petition. The resentment was real, and the community turned it into a running joke. Separately,
Jas announced that the CCC would close, then reversed it after "immense pressure". (member pages; Jas)
**Use:** a "Petition" card, where enough characters sign to make The System reverse a decree. Becoming the final boss
of a fan story suggests a badge for whichever Mod Bot is most complained about.

### The Great Mistake (Feb 2008)
A departing admin made two joke accounts admins, and two members took control of those accounts "and proceeded to
wreck the forums". The community remembers it with a mixture of horror and glee. (a member page)
**Use:** a Random Event, "The Great Mistake". A random character gets a bounded set of mod powers for one hour. Every
effect must be a layer that can be undone, so nothing a player wrote is destroyed.

### Milestone members
The member who registered just before the 5,000 mark was permabanned after one reported post, and the 5,000th member
was banned for sharing its connection. A milestone that should have been a celebration became a small cursed joke. (a
member page)
**Use:** a milestone event. When the board reaches a round number of Acts or characters, The System picks out the
character who reached it, for a reward or a punishment.

### Rupee Points: arbitrary scoring
Raie "arbitrarily awarded" Rupee Points to people who did things she liked and took them away when they didn't. (Raie)
**Use:** this is the closest thing in the wiki to Aura and The System's whims. It is worth quoting in the design as
the precedent. The game's version keeps the arbitrariness and adds the honest log.

### Joke punishments from staff
- Catchphrases from one member's page: "Ban 'em all and let God sort 'em out!", "A stupid decision to stop
  stupidity... fair enough!" and "When I say it it's true." (a member page)
- A ban for bringing up an outside dispute, and the "no outside disputes" rule it enforced. (member pages)
- Phishfest: a member stole accounts with fake log-out redirects. The community's outrage at the theft met glee at the
  response: an admin threatened an indefinite ban "for about six hours", then signed off without giving it. (member
  pages)

**Use:** the three catchphrases suit The System or a Mod Bot as they stand. "An indefinite ban for about six hours"
is a good bounded punishment: it sounds endless, but the code says six hours.

### VinnyVideo, the counterpoint
VinnyVideo saw himself as a "caretaker", with staff "more polite and even-tempered than previous administrations". A
year's ban was the heaviest penalty, except for spambots. (VinnyVideo)
**Use:** a contrast for The System: a gentler Mod Bot persona, or a quiet week when it leaves things alone.

## 2. Masks, dupes and unmasking

This section matters most for Divided World, where every character is a mask.

### The mask more popular than its owner
A newcomer account joined in Dec 2009 as an "unknown member" and became an instant icon with an advice topic and award
nominations. Staff had known all along that it was a returning member's second account; they accepted the new account
and treated the old one as the dupe. Weeks later the owner logged into the old account, and the popular mask was
permabanned. The community felt it as a small tragedy: the mask had outshone its owner, and the owner's own slip ended
it. (member pages)

**Use:** the purest model of the game's premise. A mask becomes more popular than the person behind it, the staff know
all along, and the mask falls through the owner's own slip, not through the staff. A card or Random Event could reward
a mask that becomes an "icon". Under "A response never proves a private fact", The System can play at knowing, but
nothing it says may be computed from ownership. A mask falls only by public clues or by its owner's own act.

### "Anyone who sees a Shy Guy without its mask on will die"
A game's trivia states: "It is claimed that anyone who sees a Shy Guy without its mask on will die." (Toadette Strikes)
**Use:** a Random Event line, or flavor text on the reveal mechanic. The Shy Guy suits a mascot for masked characters.

### Dupe sagas
- One banned member evaded bans under a string of new names, joke names among them, and one of his returns set off a
  flamewar.
- A dupe given the title "Member No. 6" was banned "for being an obvious dupe".
- A member who opened an "ima dupe" topic found that "nobody cared".
- One member made dupes from misspellings of his own name.
- One account was banned after 3 posts, and its page says only "INTRIGUE".
- One member ran a "Dupe School" on a splinter board, which earned a 30-day ban.
- A dupe whose only post was a capitalized greeting with its own name flooded the CCC, and was later confirmed as a
  known member.

The mood around dupes was suspicion turned into sport: spotting one was a game, and confessing one was a joke that
fell flat. (member pages)

**Use:** the "ima dupe" topic that nobody cared about is a good joke about confessing a mask. Misspelled-name dupes
suit a cosmetic mask-naming card. The greeting-spamming dupe is a ready recurring NPC: a catchphrase spammer The System
sends in. "Member No. 6" suits a title for an unmasked character. All of this is play about masks in public; none of it may involve the
system revealing who owns what.

### Night of the Living "Dupes" (Sept 15, 2007)
Someone seized inactive accounts and posted spam topics from them. The night got a horror-movie name, Night of the
Living "Dupes", which says the community took it more as a spectacle than an injury. (member pages)
**Use:** a Halloween Random Event in which dormant characters wake up and post nonsense. Their words are layers The
System adds and can take away, never posts in their owners' names.

### Hacked into a microgame
A member hacked an account and made it post offensive accusations at every member. A fangame later turned the incident
into a microgame in which the hacker holds the ban powers. The community answered an injury with mockery of the one
who caused it. (MFGG Ware; a member page)
**Use:** shows the community turning an incident into a game item. Keep the idea of a microgame in which someone grabs
the ban powers, and drop the original wording.

### Disguises
Iceman3k disguised himself as a sheep named "Darkhorn" that only posted "bleat". *(individual: owner's call)*
(Iceman3k)
**Use:** a "Sheep Disguise" card that, for a time, replaces a character's posts with "bleat", as a layer over them.

## 3. Running jokes, catchphrases and canon

### Thunder Dragon's Statue
Newcomers are warned: "do not touch Thunder Dragon's Statue; do not make a statue of anyone within 500km of Thunder
Dragon's statue". One member's page adds: "...wait, does he smite them with lightning or a Hammer?" Jaden1291 had a
multicolored statue of his own. An avatar shows a clone skidding up to touch it. In Super Mario Epic 2 a hidden room
shows Fanguy polishing a giant golden statue of Thunder Dragon. (Ah2190; Jaden1291 the Toad; Super Mario Epic 2 Dream
Machine)
**Use:** a fixture on a board or a site-wide item, "The Statue". Touching it (an Act) brings a smiting chosen by The
System, lightning or hammer. A card to build a rival statue within range of it is forbidden, which makes it tempting.

### The Walrus on member and game pages
The Walrus turns up on many pages: Revenge of the Walrus (VinnyVideo); a Walrus enemy in the DeeY engine; the
"Walruscratic" party in Stupid Political Game 2024; Groose-Strich 2016, where Walrus is the third most powerful
Politicalmon; Mors ("Mors means "walrus" in Turkish"); Trasher's admin prank "Walrus"; the Walrus in Casual Time
Battle 3's description; "friend to all walruses" (Vitiman); and Wacky Walrus World (SMB Dimensions).
**Use:** the Walrus as The System's mascot or omen: it shows up when The System is about to act. Lane A covers the
Walrus page itself.

### Let the Topic Die
"Metool says: LET THE TOPIC DIE", and the "Dead Thread's Curse" that kills his own topics. *(individual: owner's
call)* (Leon Magnus)
**Use:** a "Curse" card that makes a thread go quiet. Its way out has to be stated, for example that the next reply
lifts the curse.

it about a real person.

**Use:** a mysterious poster like xn--e5x, who drops a gift and vanishes, suits a System NPC for Random Events.

## 5. Games that became jokes, and in-jokes in games

### Thingio
"The concept of Thingio is fairly simple: there is none." It had many false release dates and an uproar "equally
compared to the LA Riots". Reviews called it a set of unpolished minigames passing itself off as one game, and the
community's ridicule turned it into a byword. (Thingio)

Its proposed Japanese name was "Getemonio", from *getemono*, "low-quality products; strange thing; strange
combination". (Thingio)
**Use:** "Thingio" as a vaporware item, or as a board name for a turnover week. "Getemonio" makes a good name for a
junk-drawer board.

### Super Mario Epic 3: the page-count ransom
The demo would release only if its topic reached ten pages in an hour. On April Fools 2007 a real demo was dressed up
as a fake .txt file called "HA YOU FEEL FOR IT", and most people didn't download it. (Super Mario Epic 3)
**Use:** a Random Event that promises a reward if a thread reaches N replies within an hour. A second event hides a
real reward in something dressed as a prank, so the characters who trust it win.

### Graveyard of notorious members
Super Mario Epic 2 has tombstones for Soveryhappy and two members "quite notorious for various reasons". Its
unlockable Room 12 is named after MFGG's Forum 12. (Super Mario Epic 2 Dream Machine)
**Use:** a graveyard board or page showing banned or retired characters as tombstones. A tombstone has to say how the
character comes back, so it never becomes a dead end.

### Members inside games
- Gameboy 4-in-1 puts members in its high score tables and has Mario singing the Sonic X theme. (Gameboy 4-in-1)
- MFGG Ware's topic filled with 30 pages of spam on announcement. Its microgames starred members: "Mecha's Case", "The
  faithful Red9378", "SOUP DA WHOOP", "Ol' British Style" and the hacking microgame above. It was abandoned after its

**Use:** microgames as cards. A card is a short, timed challenge themed on a well-known character or Act, with an
accessible path that costs the same. "Final boss" makes a badge for the character most posted about in a week.

## 6. Echoes of the Split and Minus World

- Minus World 2.0 "aims to provide a more adult-targeted forum experience compared to MFGG, with lots of meta-game
  features and self-referential humor". (Spritey) This is the closest stated precedent for Divided World's tone.
- Sides in the Split, as member pages record them: Endgame, Gato and VinnyVideo opposed it; El Huesudo II and Mario
  User favored it. Black Squirrel came back as admin shortly before it. Yoshiman "announced plans that would
  drastically reshape the very structure of MFGG". (those pages)
- "The Quest for MFGG" forum game "ended early because of The Split". (Quest for MFGG The Game)
- The narrator account of a murder-mystery event was retired after the Split. (an event account page)
- VinnyVideo's BT Forums began partly as a hangout "for people dissatisfied with MW". (VinnyVideo)
- Spinoff boards that tried to bring back the old feel: MFGG² (also known as Late Night MFGG), MFG3 and AltMFGG, made
  from summer 2012 to early 2013 "to recapture the feel of the IPB-era MFGG". (Vitiman)
- Xgoff argued that the PPP was being abused and should be replaced or joined by a board that allowed off-topic talk
  "but restricted stupidity". The CCC was the result. (Xgoff)
- MFGG 3.0 reopened in Nov 2009 with a clean slate, and banned members returned. (a member page)

**Use:** a "Clean Slate" Random Event in which The System pardons every character with an active ban. A "Split" event
divides a board in two, with characters choosing a side by an Act. Spinoff names like "Late Night", "²" and "Alt" fit
the weekly turnover's board names. "A game that ended early because of The Split" is a ready line for The System
closing an event.
