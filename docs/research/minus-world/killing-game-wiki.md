# The Minus World Killing Game, from its wiki

Record, written 2026-10-31. Three pages of Minus World's Killing Game Wiki (`wiki.minus.world`): "introduction" (the
Wayback Machine's capture of April 24, 2024), "standard_rules" and "roles" (both February 5, 2023). The owner saved
them as web archives and supplied them as "more context on killing game". The introduction is sourced from a forum
post, "The Minus World Killing Game: An Introduction", and is written in a host's voice. All three pages were last
edited in August 2018. Below is what they say, then what it suggests for hosting the game in Divided World (#37).
Wayback's header and the wiki's chrome are left out. So is one example of a "wacky" night action that names a member
and is offensive.

## What the pages say

**The pitch.** "a fast-paced do-or-die experience, in which you'll betray your friends, sacrifice some poor sap, lie
about your special role, plan murders, and engage in some courtroom drama." It sets itself against the Kira Game
before it, "almost entirely a team vs. team experience". The Killing Game "is every man for themselves": alliances
are allowed, but "it all comes down to the last man standing, and when the time comes, your partners will look mighty
ripe for the picking." It is meant to be simple. Most of the game is "your own creativity with your murders, and how
you choose to use environmental quirks and special powers to pull them off or frame others, as opposed to convoluted
rules".

**Structure** (standard rules; "Each game may have its own rules"):

- **Roles.** Every player who signs up is given one, "special abilities or restrictions". The host may allow a
  re-roll and may take requests.
- **A map.** All action takes place on it; players use it to plan their nights and to investigate.
- **A phase lasts two days:** a killing day, then an investigation day.
- **Survivors.** "Whoever survives at the end is considered the winner of the game. However the game ends is
  ultimately up to however the host runs it."

**The Killing Phase.** Once a night (unless a role says otherwise), a player may PM the host a murder: "who you want
to kill, how you want to kill them, and why you want them dead." A player may instead do something wacky: "how you
spend your night is entirely up to you." The more detailed the plan, "the more likely it'll be that you'll get away
with it".

- **One kill a night, drawn at random:** "The successful killer is selected randomly out of all submissions".
- **Witnesses come from collisions:** "if anyone tries to target the same guy as you and fails, or you pass through a
  room someone else chooses to watch, you'll have a witness on your hands."
- **Nobody may sit it out:** "you'd better have at least one guy try to murder, because if no one does, I'll lynch a
  random instead!"
- **The Boogeyman.** Every bedroom has one under the bed. When questioned, it "will crudely perform charades in order
  to show what happened in the bedroom that night". It can be fooled. It exists so that "going into someone's bedroom
  and shooting them, then walking out isn't a viable and common tactic."

**The Investigation Phase.**

- **The case is posted:** the victim is discovered, the crime scene revealed, and the case files posted.
- **Private writeups:** the host "writes up what everyone actually did at night and what they noticed". Witnesses
  "may know more than the thread lets on", and coming forward is a risk of its own.
- **Questions to the host:** players may ask about the scene and the body, and the host answers "up to a certain
  point obviously. You won't just be able to ask who the killer is."
- **Keep it in the thread:** private conversation is encouraged, but "do try to keep a decent chunk of your
  revelations and accusations in the thread. There were some people who liked to observe".

**The Class Trial.**

- **Open the whole phase.** "all your votes are public, and people would certainly be bothered if you voted for them
  while they're innocent." Whoever is in the game is on the ballot.
- **The most votes is lynched.** "if all the votes are equal, or no one votes at all, it'll go to a random lynch!"
- **Confessions don't count:** "Early confessions do not close the class trial any more. They will not be verified in
  any way and are no longer valid."
- **Suicides:** voting for a victim's suicide seems a way past a trial. So "most hosts decide to kill a random living
  player if a suicide is voted and the victim was murdered", at the host's discretion.

**Accounts.** "Never post the official account you get from the host anywhere public. This will get you immediately
kicked from the game." Players played through accounts the host issued.

**Roles.** "Some help you get away with murder more easily, some help you survive, and some help you catch the
culprit. Then you have the ones that just encourage you to dick around. I like those ones." Roles are "typically
only seen once as part of the fun is trying to guess what the extent of someone's strange abilities are". The wiki
keeps past roles as a source of ideas for new games. The capture lists one: Ultimate BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEES.

**The host's voice.** The introduction closes in persona: "Whew, I'm beat. Typing that much really knocks the wind
out of a guy. I don't even have fingers. [...] If there's any questions, I'll probably get around to answering them
here, as long as you're not a dick about it."

## What it suggests

- **The game was already played through masks.** Each player took an official account from the host and kept it
  secret, on pain of being kicked. Divided World's characters are that idea made permanent. One question this raises
  for #37: should a game deal each entrant a fresh mask for the game? If The System issued it rather than a host, no
  person would know who holds which, which the host-issued accounts never managed ("A response never proves a private
  fact").
- **The randomness has rules The System can hold:**
  - one kill a night drawn from the submissions;
  - a random lynch when nobody submits, on a tie, or when nobody votes;
  - a random death on a suicide vote.

  Each is a seeded, logged draw (#37's "roll the dice the host rolls today").
- **Witnesses are computed from collisions:** two players targeting the same victim, or a path through a watched
  room. That is the most mechanical part of a host's writeup, and it can be drafted from the submitted actions on the
  map.
- **The Boogeyman is a host-run witness in every room** that answers in charades and can be fooled. A Mod Bot persona
  could play it.
- **The map is a game object:** rooms, paths and watched rooms. It is what makes actions checkable.
- **The Class Trial is a poll with stated tie rules,** open for the whole phase. Votes are public, and an
  unverifiable confession closes nothing.
- **Spectators matter.** The host asked players to keep accusations in the thread for those watching. A Killing Game
  board is for an audience as much as for the players (#37's weekly board).
- **Roles are a canon that is rarely repeated.** A role card is new to its game, and the archive of past roles is a
  pool for new ones. That fits #55's cards dealt face down.
