What the bench shows

  • The footprint. Each occurrence keeps the width of the wider of its words and its replacement, so the post never moves when tape comes off. After a reload, an occurrence the author peeled takes its words' own width, and the residue follows it.
  • Pieces. Where the footprint crosses a line break, the tape is cut into one piece per line, and the replacement's words are shared out between them. Each piece peels on its own; the occurrence is peeled once its last piece is.
  • The peel. Hold at an end until the corner lifts, then pull back along the strip. The peel follows your hand no faster than the pace. Get too far ahead and the tape strains (it whitens and shakes), then tears: the rolled part drops, and the new ragged end has to be picked again. Let go partway and the roll waits where it is.
  • The peek. A reader drags from an end to fold the tape back and read under it; letting go lays it back down.
  • The keyboard. Holding Enter or Space picks the end, then peels at the pace. Letting go keeps what is done. It can never pull too fast, so it costs what a careful hand does.
  • Reduced motion. No fall and no shaking; a roll that comes off is gone at once, and a peek lays down at once. The pace is the same.

For the owner to judge

  • The marker: which of the four, and at what size against the board's Verdana.
  • The pace, the pick and the tear: what feels like Chuck's way, slow and fussy but never stuck.
  • The tape and the residue on each board: real enough, and readable after peeling.
  • The drop at the end: a quick fall of the rolled cylinder.
