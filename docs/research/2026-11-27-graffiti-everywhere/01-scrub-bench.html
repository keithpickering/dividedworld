Graffiti Scrub Bench

How tough a wall's paint is to wash. Today takes every coat under the sponge in one pass. Coats gives paint depth: a fresh line is laid a few coats thick, your own strokes that cross stack more, and each pass of the sponge takes one coat, thinning the paint to see-through before the post shows. Load the sample wall, switch to the sponge, and scrub.

Washing
Takes the same per pass as a hand, at Whoops!'s pace (0.55 of the box's width a second). Or hold S.

What to try

  • Sample wall, then Sponge. The red quick line is one stroke. The black block is three Fat strokes crossing, so its middle is stacked. "HELLO" is Stock. The pink heart is a second tag sprayed over the O.
  • Scrub the red line and count your passes, then scrub the black block. That difference is the "heavily worked tag takes more" rule.
  • Scrub the heart where it crosses the O. With Top tag first, the heart goes before HELLO shows any wear. With Every tag at once, both wear together.
  • Switch Washing to Today and back. Every setting replays the same strokes and washes, so you compare like for like. Unwash puts the paint back to try again.
  • Hold to scrub is the accessible path: it sweeps the box in bands and keeps going round until the wall is clean, taking a coat a pass as a hand does.

What the readout counts

  • Budget is section 4's rule: each tag's own paint, summed, against the 40% limit. A cell counts until its last coat is gone.
  • Scrubbing is the time the sponge spent moving over the wall, by hand or held.
