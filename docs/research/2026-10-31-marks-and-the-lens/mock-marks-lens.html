Marks and the Lens

Spritey's post #41 carries four plays: three Whoops! layers and one Stick Around by a character with a very long name. Nothing glass is in the page's flow. Each card kind is a mark on the post (its art, a count, and an edge that fills as it ends). Press a mark for the Lens, where every card answers the same three rows: what it is doing and how it ends, what you can do, and who did it.

View as

Desktop

1180 wide. The Lens prefers the margin beside the board, so it never covers the ink you are scrubbing.

Phone

375 wide. The Lens is a short sheet; the post is scrolled into view above it.

What to try

  1. Drag across the ink. It scrubs the top layer on your screen. The Whoops! mark's edge fills as you go, and the first-time callout goes away after your first stroke.
  2. Press a mark. The Lens opens on that card's tab. The other card is a tab beside it. Who did it waits, folded, under the controls.
  3. Hold to scrub works the top layer at the same pace as your hand, with the Lens open and the post in view.
  4. View as Spritey. The Whoops! mark gets a gold notch, and the Lens adds Wash and Write in ink.
  5. Arrive held. Stick Around takes the screen with the post inside it, ink and marks included. Open Whoops! from inside the hold, or break free.

No sentence lives on the post. A mark is art, a numeral and an edge, so a long caster or card name cannot break it, and the glass's 18px floor is kept without the glass outshouting a 12px post.

Every card fills the same three rows. State (what it does and how it ends), Act (up to two controls from a fixed kit: hold, toggle, reveal, choose, go, author), and Who (folded). A card never draws its own plate.

Marks live on the post, wherever the post is drawn, on the board or inside a takeover. More than four kinds on a post fold into "+N".
