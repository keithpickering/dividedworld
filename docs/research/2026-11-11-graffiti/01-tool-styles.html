  • The toolbar is the glass, since it's the game's tool, like the Lens and the hand. Its words are plain glass copy.
  • The cans are drawn, since they're the ink, the card's own art. The Whoops! sponge and suds work the same way: art inside the game's interface, never a third chrome.
  • The Draw button is a glass tile lifted over the ink, like the post's marks, with a can in the color you last picked.

Why D. The two-worlds design already settles who owns a control. The board is the arena, and its words and posts are the only 2003 chrome. Anything the player works is the glass. The card's art (splats, suds, the sponge, and now the cans and the ink) is the one place a drawn object lives, and it lives inside one of those two worlds, never as a third. A keeps the tribute but puts a game tool in board chrome. C is the most fun to hold but adds a look nothing else uses. D keeps the glass and gives the fun to the cans.
