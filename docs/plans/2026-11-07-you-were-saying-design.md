# You Were Saying?: the card that keeps talking

Record, written 2026-11-07. The design session with the owner, on the owner's card concept: "Extend this post's text
using a bot from 2021. The more Aura you offer, the more text is generated." Its plan is
`docs/plans/2026-11-07-you-were-saying-plan.md`. Each decision below was asked one at a time and answered by the owner,
except where a section says it is the session's own choice. The owner confirmed the whole summary before the plan was
written. The mock of the three looks weighed in section 5 is `docs/research/2026-11-07-you-were-saying/look.html`.

The owner's reason: the old voice (a real 2019–2021 base model on raw completion, self-hosted and priced at zero; The
System's design, section 6) was built for board descriptions, and the Mod Bots design (section 1) took that slot from
it and gave it to the Boss. The System's design had already named the old voice "potentially helpful for some
as-of-yet-unknown postcard effects". This is that card.

Titles weighed: You Were Saying?, Do-Go-On, Keep Talkin'. The session recommended You Were Saying?: it is a thing a
person says, and it reads as kind or cruel, which is what Pepperoni is.

## 0. Where it starts

- **The old voice is built.** `models.complete` (`supabase/functions/_shared/models/index.ts`) posts a
  `CompletionRequest` (`engine/system/model.ts`) to the instance's own OpenAI-compatible endpoint
  (`SYSTEM_COMPLETIONS_URL`, `completions.ts`), retries a transport failure, writes a ledger row, and cuts the answer with
  `cutCompletion` (`engine/system/validate.ts`). That cutter always stops at the first newline, since every slot it serves
  today is one line. A persona with `voice: 'oldVoice'` uses it for pool posts (`chatterCompletion`,
  `engine/system/chatter.ts`).
- **The scrubber is decided and not built.** The staff design (`docs/plans/2026-10-31-the-staff-design.md`, section 13):
  every old-voice output goes through a scrubber for slurs and for anything shaped like a name, email, phone number or URL;
  a hit regenerates once.
- **Cards on a post** are drawn as layers (Autocorrect's text layers, `docs/plans/2026-11-03-autocorrect-design.md`,
  section 9), with an author's saved state in `effect_state`, and a snapshot of every layer when a play lands
  (`docs/plans/2026-11-03-effect-history-design.md`).
- **The owner on ways out for a post's effect:** a timer is avoided for a single post's effect, "because somebody has to
  really dig in some cases to end up seeing a post, and a lot of the time it sort of kills a really funny gag before
  anyone can see it." Effects with wider reach can be timed. Removing it "has to be some effort for the post author". This
  card only adds; it covers nothing, which justifies its being harder to get rid of.

**New here:** an effect whose content is generated by a model, and an effect that is made again by an edit of the post.
Both are engine concepts this design adds through interfaces that name no card.

## 1. What it does

**Decided:** You Were Saying? is a **Cast on a post**, **Pepperoni**. The old voice continues the post from its last
visible character, and the continuation (the **tail**) is drawn after the post's own words. Nothing is covered, and the
author's words are never changed ("The System acts; it never destroys what a player wrote").

- **Rarity:** common, as a starting point. The owner left rarity and price undetermined; the season review sets both.
- **Settlement:** the room decides, as for every Pepperoni card (Autocorrect's design, section 8).
- **It lands** on any post with visible words (a post that is only a quote, code or images is refused in plain words,
  since there is nothing of the author's to continue).

## 2. The offer: a stepped slider

**Decided** (the owner chose the recommendation): the play form holds a slider from the base price up to a cap, in steps.
The base buys a starting length, and each step buys a fixed number of tokens more, about a sentence.

- **Economy settings:** the base price (`youWereSaying.price`), the tokens the base buys, the price of a step, the tokens
  a step buys and the most tokens in all. The starting numbers are 1.00 for 25 tokens, 0.25 for each 25 more, and 200 at
  most, so 2.75 at most in all. The season review replaces them.
- **The face** shows the price's range, as Autocorrect's does, since the length is chosen in the form.
- The tokens bought are a fact of the play, on its Act. A regeneration (section 4) is made at the same length and costs
  nothing more.

Weighed: three named sizes (a coarser offer); a free amount typed in (fiddly at odd amounts).

## 3. Generation: land at once, then type in

**Decided** (the owner chose the recommendation and added the replay):

- **The play lands at once.** Its tail is generated after it, by the server, and stored. Until the tail arrives the post
  shows the tail's cursor typing nothing, which is a real loading indicator the first time.
- **Generated once per version of the post**, never per reader. Readers read the stored text.
- **The replay** (the owner's): the tail types itself in every time the post scrolls into view, as Glitter Bomb sparkles
  on view, even long after it was generated. On the first run it is a loading indicator; on every later visit it is the
  gag, and it is what tells the author's words from the bot's.
- **The frame** (the session's own choice, not one the owner was asked): the old voice is shown a 2003 thread page (the
  thread's title, a few posts before this one, then this post under its author's name), ending exactly where the post's
  visible text ends, so a post that stops mid-sentence is continued mid-sentence. A chained tail (section 6) continues
  from the end of the tails before it. The tail may run to several lines. It stops at its token cap, or where the text
  starts a new post (a line shaped like the frame's post header). The engine's `cutCompletion` gains a stop that keeps
  newlines, for a slot that asks for it; every existing slot keeps cutting at the first newline.
- **The scrubber** (the staff design's, built here in the engine for every old-voice output): a hit regenerates once.
- **A failure refunds.** If the endpoint does not answer after its retries, or the second scrub hits, or what comes back
  is empty, the play stays (it is an Act, and it is still a play the room may react to), the Aura paid for its length
  above the base price, and the base price itself, come back to the caster's wallet as a `refund` wallet event, and the
  caster's and the author's logs say so in plain words. The card is spent; the Aura is not. On a regeneration after an
  edit, a failure keeps the tail it had, which still reads as a continuation of the words before.
- **An instance with no completion endpoint** does not offer the card: it is not sold, not offered to the pool, and a
  play of one already held is refused in plain words. An instance that switches The System off may keep the old voice,
  since the endpoint is the instance's own.

Weighed: generating before landing (the caster waits several seconds on the model, and the act function holds a slow
outbound call); a stock line standing in on failure, as for personas (a paid-for length replaced by one canned line).

## 4. Edits make it again

**Decided** (the owner's own answer): any edit of the post generates every tail on it again, at its paid length, **even
if it had been deleted**. An edit is therefore never a way to get rid of it, and every edit rolls the dice again.

- **Each generation is an append-only row**, one per play and per version of the post it continued, written by the
  server and never updated ("Append-only"). The tail drawn now is the play's generation for the post's current version.
- **A queue, newest only.** An edit queues a generation for each play on the post. A play whose post is edited again
  before its generation runs makes only the newest version's; an older one still waiting is dropped, never generated.
  A generation that returns after its version is no longer current is kept as a row of its version (History draws it,
  section 7) and does not replace the current tail.
- **Chained tails regenerate in order** (section 6): the first play's tail, then the second's from it, then the third's.

## 5. The look: terminal bleed

**Decided** (the owner chose B of three looks in the mock): the tail is drawn in **Courier, with a green tint**, as
terminal output bleeding into the post. The owner: "it fits the theme and turns it into more of a gag than a bluff,
which is better for Pepperoni." The post is on the board, in the house style, and the tail is one of the game's own
pieces, so it may carry color.

- **The cursor:** a block cursor types the tail in, and stays blinking at its end after typing finishes.
- **The glitch:** a horizontal tear, like a vsync slip, while it types. It stops once typing is done, and the blinking
  cursor carries the gag from there. (The owner left the frequency open, "less frequent or even stop altogether"; the
  session chose stop, to be judged on the shots.)
- **Reduced motion:** the tail shows at once, with no tear, and the cursor holds still.
- **Screen readers** read the tail after the post's words, announced as continued text ("Continued by You Were Saying?:
  …"), since a sighted reader sees the seam at once.

Weighed: ghost signal (the same Verdana, a faint red and cyan split; the session's recommendation, for the bluff); seamless
until it slips (identical text, given away only by the tear and the replay).

## 6. Stacking: up to three, chained

**Decided** (the owner chose the recommendation): up to three plays per post. Each later play continues from the end of
the text before it, the earlier tails included, so the bot keeps going. They are drawn as one run of terminal text, each
tail its own layer.

## 7. History

**Decided** (the owner chose the recommendation, and raised the need): the effect history design's views take in the
new concept of an effect that is made again by edits.

- **History** (the post's list of versions) draws each version with the tails generated for it, in full, ignoring
  deletion. Each re-roll shows beside the edit that caused it.
- **As it landed** draws the version current when the play landed, with that version's tails, and with each tail's
  deletion as it stood then, from the snapshot.
- **Before snapshots** and the rest of that design's rules hold unchanged.

Weighed: As it landed and now only, with History showing the author's words alone (a funny re-roll lost from view).

## 8. The way out: delete it all, or it comes back

**Decided** (the owner's own answer, refining the recommendation):

- **The hold:** a pointer held down anywhere on the tail block, or Backspace held while the tail block has focus, erases
  from the end, about 6 characters a second (a screen setting). The same hold and the same rate for mouse, touch and
  keyboard ("An accessible path costs what the obstacle costs"). The tail block is one focusable control, announced with
  how it is erased: "Hold to erase. It comes back if you stop."
- **It comes back:** let go for about two seconds before it is all gone, and it types itself back in, completely. The only
  way to stop it is to erase the whole thing, every chained tail on the post, in one go.
- **Only a clear is saved.** Partial progress is never saved. When the author erases the last character, the clear is
  saved in the play's `effect_state` for the post's current version, and it holds for everyone. Any other reader may erase
  it on their own screen, and it is back on refresh.
- **An edit brings it back** (section 4): the new version's tails have no clear.
- **The cursor is not the control.** The owner: the cursor moves as the delete happens, so the hold is on the tail block,
  not on the cursor.

Its cost is the length the caster paid for: more Aura is more to erase. Weighed: a random fight-back while erasing, which
the owner replaced with this; a steady erase with saved progress; word by word with a confirm.

## 9. Where the tail shows

**Decided** (the session's own choice, as Autocorrect's section 7 decided for its strips): only on the post itself.
Quotes, alert excerpts, thread previews, search and The System's digests read the author's words alone. A Quote copies
the original. The tail is never baked into anything else.

## 10. Mod Bots

**Decided** (the session's own choice): a Mod Bot may play it, at the base length, from the pool's card offer. It suits a
personality core that "doesn't work quite right" (the Mod Bots design, section 1).

## 11. Engine and instance

- **Engine:**
  - **A card that writes** (`CardRules.writes`): a card declares that its effect holds generated text, the tokens a play
    bought (from its configuration), and its frame, a pure function from the post's visible text, the thread's title and
    a few posts before it, the tails before it, and the instance's settings, to a `CompletionRequest`'s prompt and stops.
  - The generation queue, its worker and its rows; the regeneration on edit; the refund on failure.
  - The scrubber, for every old-voice output, and the newline-keeping stop in `cutCompletion`.
  - The text layer after a post's body (an append layer), beside the text layers over it.
  - History's and As it landed's drawing of generated text.
  - A ledger row for a model call made for a play, not for a staff member or a meeting.
  - Nothing names a card.
- **Instance** (`instances/divided-world/`): the card's rules, its frame's words, its effect (the terminal bleed, the
  replay, the hold), its form, its face, its log words, and the scrubber's word list.

## 12. The masks

- The generation reads the post's public text, the thread's public posts and the public tails. Nothing it reads, and
  nothing its timing depends on, is who owns anything.
- A refund is a wallet event of the caster's own; the public sees only that the tail did not come.
- An author's clear is a save by the post's author, public as every effect state is.
- The time a generation takes depends on the post's text, the tokens bought and the endpoint, the same for every caster.

## 13. The log

- **The caster:** "You played You Were Saying? on post N for M tokens" (the existing play line, with the length).
  On a failure: "The old voice didn't answer for your You Were Saying? on post N, so your 1.75 came back."
- **The author:** "You Were Saying? on your post continued it", and on each edit "Your edit of post N made its You Were
  Saying? again". A clear: "You erased You Were Saying? on post N". A failure: "You Were Saying? on post N didn't take."

## 14. Ways out

- **A tail:** the author erases it all, every chained tail, in one hold; any reader erases it on their own screen; History
  and As it landed always show the author's words. No timer, by the owner's rule for single-post effects.
- **A waiting generation:** it runs, fails and refunds, or is dropped for a newer version. It never waits on a player.
- **The play:** a finished Act.

## 15. A busy instance

- A generation is one completion call per play per version of a post, at most 200 tokens, and only for posts that carry
  the card. Edits on carded posts are what make calls, and a queued call for an older version is dropped, so a run of
  edits makes one call per play, not one per edit.
- The completion endpoint is the instance's own, priced at zero per token and recorded per GPU second, so cost grows with
  plays and edits on carded posts, both bounded by Aura spent.
- A page reads its tails with its other effect states, in one batched read, never one per post.
- The worker takes jobs in batches, the oldest first, so a slow endpoint lengthens the wait and never stalls the board.

## Open

- **The tear's frequency** after typing, judged on the shots.
- **The Strategy Guide's copy** for the card is committed and approved by the owner in review.
