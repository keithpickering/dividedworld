## The Metagame

Divided World looks like a message board from 2003, and it is one. It is also a game, and every post on it is a move.

You are a **player**. You sign in to one account and play up to **six characters**, each wearing a mask. Nobody else
knows which masks are yours, and the board will never tell them. Each character has a secret **class**: a way of
playing the crowd that decides which reactions make it stronger and which wear it down.

Everything anyone does here is a public **Act**: a post, an edit, a reaction, a card played on someone's post, a Turn,
a ruling from The System. Any Act can be reacted to, and any Act can be spun out into a thread of its own.

The game runs in **seasons** of about three months (three weeks during the playtest). A character earns **Aura** all
season by drawing the reactions its class favors. At the season's end, The System posts the final standings in a big
public finale, and every character carries on into the next season.

Here's the important part: **nobody is paid for guessing anybody's class.** Figuring out who's a Heel is a crowd
sport, not a scoring event. Knowing someone's act just tells you how to play along with it, or how to mess with it. An
open secret is a perfectly good strategy, and so is a subtle one.

The board is the arena. Behind the glass, along the bottom of every page, is your **HUD**: the character you're playing
as, your orbs, and your hand of cards. The glass is out of character and always tells you the truth. The board, and
anything The System says on it, might not.

## Reactions

Every Act except a reaction can take reactions. There are sixteen, in four groups of four, and the picker lays them out
like a map:

|              | **Negative**                     | **Positive**                     |
| ------------ | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Excited**  | 🍅 😡 🤬 👎 **Heat**             | 🔥 ❤️ 🙌 🤩 **Hype**             |
| **Calm**     | 💤 🙄 🗑️ 🥱 **The Groan**         | 😌 ☕ 🤝 🧘 **The Nod**           |

That 2×2 grid is the whole game. Every class earns from some corners of it and bleeds from others (see
[Classes](#classes)).

**The rules of reacting:**

- **Up to three reactions per post, per player.** That's three in total across all of your characters, not three per
  mask. The picker shows how many you have left ("2 of 3 left").
- **Your reactions share one vote.** Leave one reaction and it carries your whole vote. Leave three and each carries a
  third. A 🔥 alone is a full cheer. A 🔥, a ☕ and a 🍅 together are a shrug in three directions.
- **Your characters can't react to each other.** A post by one of your own masks shows its tallies and no React button.
- **Who reacted is never shown.** Not to the poster, not to anyone. Tallies are public; reactors are not.
- **The first post counts most.** Within one settlement window (a day), your vote on a character's first Act counts in
  full, your vote on its second Act counts half, its third a quarter, and so on.

**Strategy:** Pick reactions on purpose. If you want a post to land, leave one reaction, not three. And if you're the
one posting, the halving rule means one great post a day is worth more from each reader than five okay ones.

## Aura

Aura is measured in **orbs**, and you can hold up to six. It shows as six orbs in your HUD.

Aura comes from other players' reactions, read through your character's class. **The System never pays Aura**, so
there's no way to farm the robot. You earn by performing for people.

### Two kinds of Aura

- **Your wallet** is shared by all your characters and seen only by you. Every character earns into it and spends from
  it, so a veteran can open a brand-new mask with a full wallet and a plan. It **caps at 6.00 orbs**. Aura you earn past
  the cap leaves the wallet, so spend before you're full.
- **Each character's standing** is the Aura it earned this season. It's public, it can't be spent, and it keeps
  counting past your wallet's cap. Standing is what shows on the character's postbit as a **rank** with a row of pips,
  and it resets into a season record when the season ends.

### The daily settlement

Aura moves for everyone else once a day, at the **settlement** (in the small hours across the Americas). Between
settlements, your orbs show a hatched **ghost fill**: a live estimate of where you're headed. Favored reactions grow it,
and hostile ones drain solid fill to a pale band. At the settlement the ghost turns solid, orb by orb.

The live estimate values each reaction as a whole vote on its own ("up to +0.10"). The settlement does the real math,
with shared votes and halving, so expect the solid fill to come in a little under the ghost.

### Your log

**My log** is the honest record. Grouped by settlement, it lists every line that moved your Aura, which Act did it,
which corner of the grid, and how much, in plain words. It never says who reacted. If you're ever unsure whether
something was done to you or just said to you, the log is the answer.

### The Aura Legend

Starting numbers, all of them tuned each season. One "vote" is one player's whole reaction vote on one Act.

| What happens                                                           | Aura         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| A vote in your class's favored corner, on your post or card            | +0.10        |
| A vote in your class's weak corner, on your post or card               | −0.10        |
| Each further Act by the same character in a window                     | half the one before |
| Wallet cap                                                             | 6.00         |
| Class bonuses and penalties                                            | see [Classes](#classes) |

### Ranks

A character's rank comes from its settled standing this season. The names are placeholders for now:

| Standing | Rank               |
| -------- | ------------------ |
| 0.00     | Jobber             |
| 1.00     | Enhancement Talent |

## Classes

A class is a playstyle: a real way people take part in a forum. Pick the one that fits how you already post, or take
one on as a challenge. New seasons may bring new classes.

One rule ties every class to the cards: **reactions to a card you play count for you, by your class.** A Heel's
Whoops! that draws 🍅 pays the Heel. A Face's card that draws 🔥 pays the Face.

| Class          | Earns from                                    | Hurt by                                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Face**       | 🔥 Hype                                       | 💤 The Groan; upstaging others            |
| **Heel**       | 🍅 Heat                                       | ☕ The Nod (the polite shrug)             |
| **Jabroni**    | 💤 The Groan                                  | 🔥 Hype (being taken sincerely)           |
| **Technician** | ☕ The Nod                                    | 🍅 Heat (getting dragged in)              |
| **Lurker**     | Calling the room early; spun threads; cards   | Posting too much                          |

### Face

The crowd's favorite.

- **+0.10** per vote of Hype on its posts and cards.
- **Lifting a post: +0.10** per vote of Hype a post draws after the Face played a Sweet card on it (someone else's post).
- **−0.10** per vote of the Groan.
- **Stealing thunder: −0.20** when a card the Face played on someone else's post draws more votes than the post did.
  Upstaging is unbecoming.

**Strategy:** Be the hype man. A Sweet card on a post that's about to take off pays you for every cheer it gets
afterward, but if your card gets more love than the post, you pay for it. Keep your card's message warm, not funnier
than the post.

### Heel

The one you love to hate.

- **+0.10** per vote of Heat on its posts and cards.
- **Stealing thunder: +0.20** when a Salty card the Heel played on someone else's post draws more votes than the post.
- **−0.10** per vote of the Nod.

**Strategy:** Heat is income, so anger is a gift. Your real enemy is the calm, polite reply, so anyone who's figured
you out will start leaving ☕ on everything you post. A Salty card with a sharper line than the post it lands on is the
Heel's signature move.

### Jabroni

Anti-comedy. Posts built to bomb, and the win is the groan.

- **+0.10** per vote of the Groan on its posts and cards.
- **Selling the L: +0.20** for every Salty card that lands on its posts.
- **−0.10** per vote of Hype.

**Strategy:** Bait the Salty cards. Every Whoops! thrown at a Jabroni costs the caster at least an orb and pays the
Jabroni 0.20 for taking it. The danger is someone who finds you genuinely funny.

### Technician

The respected regular. The effort poster whose answers get nods rather than screams.

- **Respected: +0.10** per vote of the Nod on its posts and cards.
- **Evergreen: +0.05 more** per vote of the Nod on a post of its own once that post is at least 3 days old (posts only,
  not cards).
- **Dragged in: −0.10** per vote of Heat.

**Strategy:** Write the reply people bookmark. Old posts that keep getting ☕ keep paying. The Technician and the Heel
are mirror images: each one's income is the other's weakness.

### Lurker

Reads the room, and wins by barely saying anything.

- **Calling it: +0.10** for an early reaction (among the first three players to react to a post) that matches where the
  post's reactions end up. It's paid once at least three other players have reacted, and judged without your own vote.
  It's paid by your share of your vote, so hedging across corners pays only the part that landed.
- **Spinning a thread: +0.20** when a thread you spun from someone else's Act draws replies from three or more other
  players.
- **+0.10** per vote of any kind on a card the Lurker played.
- **Posting too much: −0.10** for every post beyond three in a window. Cards, reactions and reveals never count as
  posts.

**Strategy:** Commit early with a single reaction. Hedged calls pay a fraction. Spin threads from the Acts everyone's
already looking at. Cards are a Lurker's voice: any reaction to one pays, whatever the corner.

### Griefing, and the counter

If you think you know someone's class, you can react against it: tea the Heel, cheer the Jabroni, yawn at the Face.
That's allowed, and it's half the fun.

The counter is the **Turn**. Picture a thread that says "X is a secret Heel" and fills up with ☕. Then X quietly Turns
Technician, and every polite shrug aimed at them becomes Aura.

## Turns and Reveals

### Turns

A character can change class once every **30 days** (every 7 during the playtest). A Turn is the game's storytelling
move, and the way to rescue a performance that isn't working. It takes effect at the **next settlement**, so the window
you're in is scored under your old class, and you can cancel it until then.

You choose how loud it is:

- **Silent:** nobody is told.
- **Announced:** "Pipewrench has Turned," and nothing more.
- **Announced with the old class:** "Pipewrench, a Heel since August, has Turned."
- **Announced with the new class**, or with both. Naming the new class is a reveal (below).

Announced Turns are posted in the season's Turns thread on The System's board.

### Reveals

Nothing in the game ever forces a class into the open. Not a card, not The System, not the season's end. **Only you
reveal a class, and only when you choose.**

A **reveal** is an Act your character makes, in any thread you like, with up to 200 characters of its own words. It
carries a checked badge: The System confirms the class at that moment. That's the difference between a verified reveal
and a character just *saying* what it is.

A reveal is a dated fact ("Revealed: Heel (Oct 3)"). If you Turn silently afterward, the badge doesn't change, because
it was true on Oct 3 and still is. An announced Turn afterward shows "Has Turned since." Revealing earns nothing extra
and costs nothing directly. The cost is that everyone can now play against a known class.

You can also reveal a class when you create a character, or as part of an announced Turn.

### New characters

A new character can **drop now** (it can act immediately, and its first post is its entrance) or **debut at the next
settlement**. Either way it can be silent, announced, or announced with its class. Dropping a mask the same hour a
story breaks is a real move. Profiles show only the month a character joined, never the time.

### The season finale

At the end of a season, The System posts every character in order of final standing, with its place, rank and
standing, plus whatever its player made public that season: its reveals and its announced Turns. **Classes nobody
revealed stay secret into the next season.** "How did that guy get so much Aura being such a jerk?" is a clue, not a
stated fact.

## Cards

A post is a move. A card is a riposte or a hype-up, a way to play off someone else's move.

You buy cards at the **Vending Machine** with orbs from your wallet. They go into your **hand**, shared by all your
characters. To play one, open the hand in your HUD and arm a card. Every place it can land lights up with a ghost mark.
Pick one, add a message of up to 200 characters, and play it as your current character. A riposte with no words falls
flat.

Playing a card is an Act of its own. It shows on the post as a **mark**, which opens the **Lens**, where anyone can read
it, react to it and spin from it. A sharp card can steal a post's thunder.

### Alignments

- **Sweet:** helps the post or character it lands on.
- **Salty:** gets in its way.
- **Pepperoni:** depends entirely on context.

### Plays

Each card is played one way, shown on its face:

- **Cast:** played on a target (a post, a character or a thread, depending on the card).
- **Use:** played on your own post or character.
- **Set:** played **face down** on your own post. Everyone sees the same card back, so nobody can tell a trap from a
  bluff.

### Rarity

Cards are **common**, **uncommon**, **rare** or **legendary**. Rarity decides what a card can reach (a character from
uncommon up, a thread from rare up, a whole board only as a legendary), what a trap can catch, and what a ward can turn
away.

### Stacking

A post carries one of each card at a time, except Whoops!, which stacks up to three. A face-down card is one per post.

### The cards so far

| Card             | Rarity   | Alignment | Play                   | Price                          | What it does |
| ---------------- | -------- | --------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------ |
| **Stick Around** | Common   | Sweet     | Cast on a post         | 1.00                           | Once the post settles into view, the reader is pulled back to it until they check a box in the caster's own words ("I legally certify that I have read this post in full and thought about it contemplatively") or break free. |
| **Whoops!**      | Common   | Salty     | Cast on a post         | 1.00, plus 0.25 per 500 characters of the post when played (3.00 at most) | Splats the post in ink of the caster's color. Readers can scrub it, but only on their own screen. Only the author's strokes are saved, so the author can wash it clean or draw in it. |
| **Bluff**        | Common   | Pepperoni | Set                    | 0.25                           | A card back that does nothing at all. |
| **Trap Card**    | Uncommon | Salty     | Set                    | 1.50                           | Springs on the next common or uncommon card cast on the post: catches it, and pays the trapper what the caster paid to play it. |
| **Force Field**  | Uncommon | Sweet     | Use on your own post   | 1.50                           | For a week, turns away every common or uncommon card cast on the post. |
| **Glitter Bomb** | Uncommon | Pepperoni | Cast on a character    | 1.50                           | Every post by that character sparkles for a week. |
| **Main Event**   | Rare     | Sweet     | Cast on a thread       | 3.00                           | Pins the thread to the top of its board for a day. |

### Traps and bluffs

A face-down card lasts **7 days**. It either springs once or quietly ends, and an ending reveals nothing: a Bluff stays
a bluff.

When a Trap Card springs, it posts a public Act naming the card it caught, and the caught card is spent. The trapper
gets back exactly what the caster paid **at the moment of playing**, not what the card cost at the machine. Today only
Whoops! has a play-time cost (its length charge), so a Trap Card only pays out in orbs against a Whoops!. Against
anything else it's a clean stop. The spring is an Act like any other, and its reactions pay the trapper by class.

A trap only catches cards **at or below its own rarity**. A rarer card sails over it and lands as normal, and the trap
stays set.

**Strategy:** A card back costs a Bluff 0.25 to fake. Is that one real? A long post with a card back on it is the most
expensive place in the game to throw a Whoops!.

### Playing on your own masks

Nothing stops you from playing a card on a post by one of your own other characters. A staged feud between your own
masks is legal, but it costs real orbs, and no trap or payout ever lets you come out ahead playing against yourself.
Only other players' reactions to it are worth anything.

## The Masks

The board never tells anyone who's behind a mask, and it never lets anyone *measure* it. Who reacted, who owns which
character, a character's class: none of it is shown, counted or hinted at by anything the system says.

What characters do in public, though, is fair game, and the game doesn't hide it. **Detective work is part of play.**
Things a sharp reader can notice:

- When a character posts, edits, reacts or Turns.
- When a character's avatar was uploaded.
- A mask that dropped the hour a story broke.
- Writing style, posting hours, and who is never online at the same time as whom.
- **"Users browsing this thread"**: characters appear the moment they arrive, and drop off about five minutes after they
  go quiet. If one mask goes quiet just as another appears, people may notice. (You never see Superman and Clark Kent in
  the same room.)
- Thread view counts count characters, so one player reading through three masks counts three times.

That's deduction, never proof. If you'd rather a mask not show up in "Users browsing," each character has a **Hide my
online status** switch in My characters.

**Editing** is public: every edit is an Act with a visible history. You get a two-minute grace period to fix a typo
without the post saying "Last edited."

## The System

The System runs the board. Each season it's played by a **Boss**: a staff character with its own name, profile, voice
and hobbies, who treats the board as its toybox and your characters as its action figures. It reports on good
performances, mocks bad ones, demands the first and punishes the second. Its profile is written in character and may
bluff. Its grudges land on masks, never on the person behind them, because it can't know who that is either.

The Boss's actions are **moves**, and every move works the same way:

- **An effect**, chosen from a fixed list the code limits (renaming a board, for example).
- **A demand**, something the board can do that ends the move early.
- **An expiry**, so every move ends anyway.

Meeting a demand reverts the move and raises the Boss's opinion of whoever met it. The glass shows each move in force
plainly: what it did, its demand and how close the board is, and when it expires.

**A Boss changes over a season.** It arrives on its best behavior in a **honeymoon** of about two weeks, settles into
its **reign**, and spends the last three weeks in a **meltdown**: more moves, harsher ones. At the handover, the
outgoing Boss names its successor, and the new Boss starts with no opinions of anyone. Impress the boss on its first
day.

The System's own board holds each night's results and each season's finale. You can't post there, but you can spin a
thread from anything it says.

## Coming Attractions

These are designed but not built yet, so details may change before they arrive:

- **Mod Bots:** the Boss's henchmen. They visit posts that break the rules, and any reader can try to intercept a visit
  before the bot files its report, by answering its dumb quiz, paying its bribe, or distracting it with a card.
- **Rules and the warn bar:** arbitrary rules that never stop you posting but fill a character's warn bar, ending in a
  timed ban, with the way out stated in your log.
- **Reports and appeals:** snitching is public and costs Aura. Appeals are one shot, heard by the bot that punished you.
- **Minus Balls and Random Events:** things that appear on a page load. SOMETHING HAPPENED!
- **Locks and petitions:** a thread on thin ice locks, and the board can pay to open it again.
- **Feuds, matches and the season's finale battle.**
- **More cards, packs and drops.** Not every card will be sold by the machine forever.

## Conclusion

That's the game as it stands. This is a pre-alpha, and every number above is a starting point the season review will
replace with what the cast actually does. Bookmark this guide: it'll change as the game does.

The game may be unfair. It will never be a trap. Every state your character can be put in tells you how it ends, and
if you ever find one that doesn't, tell an admin.
