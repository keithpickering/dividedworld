# Card art: the engine's contract

Current. This is what the engine holds a card's art to; the build enforces it with `tools/check-card-art.mjs`, whose
self-test is `tools/test-card-art.mjs`. The designs behind it are `docs/plans/2026-11-05-card-template-design.md`
(section 6) and `docs/plans/2026-11-11-rendered-card-art-design.md`, which let a card's own canvas be a raster image;
both are records.

## An art set

Every card has an art set, one folder per card in an instance: `instances/<name>/ui/cards/<key>/`. Plain files directly
in `ui/cards/` are not folders and the guard does not read them. Nor does it read a folder there whose name could not
be a card's key (lowercase words joined by hyphens), such as the `__pycache__` Python leaves when a scene is run: that
is a tool's leavings, not a card.

- **The canvas**, the card's picture: `art.svg` or `art.webp`, exactly one.
  - **`art.svg`** is vector, so it is sharp from the smallest size to the largest. Its root `svg` has a viewBox of
    exactly `0 0 540 840`. The art fills the whole canvas; a design that keeps to one area puts its subject in the focus
    zone on a transparent ground, because the engine always draws the card's dark ground and a soft glow in the card's
    color under it. The zone is a guide, never a mask.
  - **`art.webp`** is a raster image, for painted or rendered art: a still WebP of exactly 1080 by 1680 pixels (twice
    the canvas, sharp at the largest size on a 3× screen), at most 400 KB. It fills the canvas and covers the ground
    under it. One file serves all four sizes.
- **`mark.svg`**, the mark. A square viewBox. A silhouette drawn to read at 18px in a post's mark tile. It is required,
  not a fallback to the canvas, because a full-art canvas shrunk to 18px is mud.
- **`art.json`**, the manifest: `credit` (who made it) and `license` (the terms they offer it under), both non-empty
  strings. The credit shows on the sheet's foot line only, as "Art · <credit>". It names a real person and describes the
  art, never a character.
- The card's color, ink and tagline live in the instance's `CardUi`, not in the folder.

```json
{ "credit": "Divided World", "license": "CC0 1.0 Universal: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/" }
```

## The template's numbers

`engine/ui/cards/template.ts` holds `TEMPLATE`. Lengths are in card units on a card of `card` 600 by 900 (2:3); the
stylesheet draws each as a fraction of the card's own width, so the same face is right at every size.

| Field | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TEMPLATE.card` | 600 by 900 | The whole card. |
| `TEMPLATE.border` | 30 | The frame's thickness, all the way round. |
| `TEMPLATE.outerRadius` | 45 | The card's corner radius (`border` plus the canvas radius). |
| `TEMPLATE.canvas` | x 30, y 30, 540 by 840, radius 15 | The art's area. The art's own coordinates are 0 0 540 840. |
| `TEMPLATE.keepClear` | 12 | No piece is drawn outside the line 12 units in from the card's outer edge. |
| `TEMPLATE.focus` | x 70, y 100, 460 by 400 | The focus zone, in card units. Optional, never a mask. |
| `TEMPLATE.plateTop` | 600 | The plate zone runs from here to the canvas's bottom. Keep the lower part of the art as texture behind the plates. |

`SIZES` holds the four widths in pixels, each a whole-pixel 2:3: `slot` 86, `phone` 104, `fan` 150, `sheet` 276.

## The template file

`engine/ui/cards/card-template.svg` is what an artist works from: a locked guides layer (the frame, the canvas, the
keep-clear line, the focus zone and the plate zone) over an empty art layer. It is generated by `templateSvg()` in
`engine/ui/cards/template.ts`, and a test fails when the committed file differs from what the module generates.

## What `tools/check-card-art.mjs` holds

Run `node tools/check-card-art.mjs [--root <directory>]`. For every card folder of every instance:

1. There is exactly one canvas, `art.svg` or `art.webp`, and `mark.svg` and `art.json` are there.
2. `art.svg`'s root viewBox is four numbers, exactly `0 0 540 840`. `art.webp` is a WebP image (its header says so, and
   its first chunk is `VP8 `, `VP8L` or `VP8X`), exactly 1080 by 1680, and not animated.
3. `mark.svg`'s viewBox is square.
4. `art.json` parses, and `credit` and `license` are non-empty strings.
5. Size caps: `art.svg` 200 KB, `art.webp` 400 KB, `mark.svg` 20 KB, `art.json` 2 KB.
6. Nothing in either SVG runs or reaches out, read case-insensitively over the whole file: no `<script`,
   `<foreignObject` or `<image`; no attribute whose name starts with `on`; no `@import`; every `href`, `xlink:href` and
   `url(` value starts with `#`.

Beyond that list, for a hostile file: no `<!DOCTYPE` or `<!ENTITY` (an entity can expand to a URL after the reading above);
no `xml:base`; no `<set>` or `<animate...>` whose `attributeName` is an href or `xml:base`; and no numeric character
reference (`&#x6a;`, `&#106;`) inside an attribute value, since it hides a character from the reading.

**What it does not catch:** a CSS escape in a `<style>` (`u\72l(`), a `values` or `to` computed some other way, anything
a viewer does with a `data-` attribute, and a character reference in a `<style>` that spells `url(`. None of these runs
inside an `<img>`, which is how a card's SVG is shown: a browser runs no script there and loads nothing. The guard is
the second wall, for the template's other readers (an artist's editor, a raw view on GitHub). Text and comments are read
as markup, deliberately: a false positive is cheap and a miss is not.

A WebP runs nothing and fetches nothing, so it has no such reading. The guard reads its header and its length and
decodes nothing, so it does not catch a file whose header is right and whose image data is corrupt: the browser then
draws no picture, and the card's name and rules still say everything, since the art is decoration. Metadata chunks in an
extended file (EXIF, XMP) are not read; they are inert in an `<img>`.

The guard reads only the files named here. Anything else in a card's folder is the instance's, such as the scene a
rendered picture was made from.

## The card back

A back says nothing about the card it is, so its look is the instance's and the same for every card. Without anything
from the instance, `PostcardBack` draws the stylesheet's back: smoke in the glass's colors and, where the instance gives
one, its mark (`InstanceUi.cardBack.mark`) at the center.

An instance may give the back its picture, in one folder, `instances/<name>/ui/card-back/`:

- **`art.svg` or `art.webp`**, exactly one: the back of every card (`InstanceUi.cardBack.canvas`).
- **`art.json`**, the manifest, as a card's. A back has no foot line, so the credit is shown nowhere; it is there so the
  art's terms are with the art.
- No `mark.svg`: the back's mark is the instance's own, and a back has no tile of its own.

The picture is a canvas as a card's is, held by the guard to the same rules: an SVG on `0 0 540 840` that runs and
fetches nothing, or a still WebP of exactly 1080 by 1680, at most 400 KB. A back with its picture draws that on the
canvas, as a face draws its art, and no mark over it: the picture holds the emblem. The frame is the engine's either way.

A pack's hit pull has no back of its own. A gold back shown only while a pack opens would suggest that a card set face
down could look different, so every back is the same back, and the hit is told only once it is turned.

The small tile of a face-down card on a post is not given the picture. It keeps the stylesheet's smoke: a 1080 by 1680
image decoded for an 18px tile, once for every face-down card on a page, is a cost with nothing to show for it.

The design is `docs/plans/2026-11-11-rendered-card-back-design.md`, a record; it gave the hit pull a second picture,
which the owner took out the same day.

## A variant is the same canvas

A variant of a card is another canvas of the same size, not a different frame: an SVG on the same `0 0 540 840`
viewBox, or a WebP of 1080 by 1680, held to the rules above for a card's own canvas. Who owns a variant, how it is found
and how a played one shows are the variant design's, which is not written yet. The mark stays an SVG.
